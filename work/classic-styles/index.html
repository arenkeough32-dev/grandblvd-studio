Our work · Case study

Classic Styles Salon & Day Spa: a family salon on Grand Blvd, rebuilt to win its best clients.

Classic Styles is a family salon in New Port Richey, run by a stylist with more than 35 years behind the chair. It’s also the shop we work out of, and the first site we built. Here’s what we changed, and why.

39pages live, each one answering a question clients ask
2languages: a full English and Spanish site
5nearby-town pages: Holiday, Trinity, Port Richey, Hudson, Odessa
5.0stars on Google from 52 reviews

Counts taken from the live site and its Google profile on 9 November 2026. The reviews are the salon’s own; we didn’t write or buy any of them.

The problem

The old homepage opened with prices for everyday cuts. That told a first-time visitor “budget salon”, when the work the salon is proudest of, and earns the most from, is lived-in blonde, color correction and bridal hair. People searching for those services had no page that spoke to them.

What we built

  • A first screen that sells the best work. Premium services and a free consultation come first, with the stylists’ experience right under them.
  • Call and text buttons that never leave the screen. On a phone, booking is always one tap away, all the way down the page.
  • Real galleries for every stylist. Real client photos, credited to the stylist who did the work. No stock photos.
  • A full Spanish site. Six pages in Spanish, so Spanish-speaking clients in Pasco can read and book in their own language.
  • A page for every service: balayage, color correction, extensions, cuts, perms, nails, weddings and gift certificates.
  • Pages for the towns next door. Clients drive in from Holiday, Trinity, Port Richey, Hudson and Odessa. Each town now has its own page, so the salon can show up when people there search.
  • Straight answers to real questions. Twelve short guides, like what balayage costs in New Port Richey or how long a perm lasts. These are the questions people type into Google and ask AI assistants.

Built to be found, and fast

Every page carries the details Google reads to understand a local business: name, address, phone, hours, services and reviews, all matching the salon’s Google profile. In an independent mobile test (Google’s own speed and quality check, median of three runs), the homepage scores:

The Google profile

We went through the salon’s Google Business Profile line by line: categories, services with prices, description, hours and website link. Most of it was already right. The one thing missing is a real photo of the storefront, which is next on the list. We also found an old duplicate listing on another map site with the wrong address and hours, and flagged it to be fixed.
