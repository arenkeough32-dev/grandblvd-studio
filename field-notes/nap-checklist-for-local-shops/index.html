Field notes

The one-page NAP checklist: keeping your shop's name, address and phone the same everywhere

NAP is just "name, address, phone." It sounds too simple to matter, but Google uses how consistently those three things appear across the internet as one of its signals for whether to trust and rank a local business. A shop with five slightly different versions of its own address looks, to Google, like five different shops. Here's where to check.

Why a small mismatch is a real problem

"4116 Grand Blvd" and "4116 Grand Boulevard, Suite 1" are the same place to a person and two different places to a lot of local-search software. The same goes for an old phone number still sitting on one directory, or a shop name with "LLC" on one listing and not another. None of it looks dishonest. It just makes the signal weaker everywhere it shows up.

Pick one exact version first

Before touching any listing, write down the exact name, address (with or without a suite number, spelled out the same way every time) and phone number you want to use everywhere. Use what's on your lease or utility bill as the source of truth for the address. Then go copy that exact text into every listing below — don't retype it from memory each time.

Where to check it

  1. Google Business Profile — the one that matters most for map search.
  2. Your website footer — people and Google both read it there.
  3. Facebook Page — the About section, not just a post.
  4. Instagram bio — if you list an address or phone there.
  5. Your booking app's public profile (Vagaro, GlossGenius, Fresha, Booksy) — these often get overlooked and show up in their own search results.
  6. Apple Maps / Apple Business Connect — a separate listing from Google, worth claiming on its own.
  7. Yelp, even if you never log in to it — if a listing already exists, an old address will sit there until someone claims and fixes it.
  8. Nextdoor and any local chamber of commerce or Pasco County business directory you're listed in.

A note on old listings

If you moved, renamed, or changed numbers in the last few years, assume at least one of the above still has the old information, because almost every shop does. It's worth the half hour to go check, not guess.

If this feels like a lot

It's the kind of work that's boring to do and easy to let slide, which is exactly why a lot of shops in New Port Richey and Pasco County never get to it. It's part of the ongoing Google profile care we do for shops on a monthly plan, alongside posts and review replies.
