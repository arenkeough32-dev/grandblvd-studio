A Google Business Profile checklist for Pasco County salons and barbershops

When someone searches "hair salon near me" in New Port Richey, almost everything that decides whether you show up happens on your Google Business Profile, not your website. Most shops have one, half-finished, from years ago. Here's what to check, in order.

1. Name, address and phone match everywhere

Your profile name should be your real shop name, nothing added for keywords. Your address should be typed exactly the same way on your profile, your website footer and your Facebook page: same abbreviations, same suite number, every time. A phone number that rings your shop, not a tracking number that changes. Small mismatches are one of the quietest reasons a shop stops showing up in the map results.

2. Categories, then services

Pick the primary category that's the closest real match to what you do most (Hair Salon, Barber Shop, Nail Salon, Day Spa) rather than the broadest one. Add every honest secondary category that fits. Then fill in the services list itself, with a price range for each if you're comfortable sharing one, and a photo for every service you can.

3. Hours, including holidays

Set your regular hours and then set special hours for every holiday you close or shorten, as far ahead as Google lets you. "Hours may differ" under a holiday is one of the most common reasons a customer drives to a closed shop and leaves a one-star review that has nothing to do with your haircut.

4. Photos: real ones, dated recently

Exterior (so people recognize the building from the street), interior, your team, and real work — with the client's OK. Skip stock photos; Google and customers can both tell. Add a few new ones every month so the profile doesn't look abandoned.

5. Attributes that actually apply to you

Walk-ins welcome, appointment required, wheelchair accessible, women-owned, LGBTQ+ friendly, Spanish spoken — whatever is true. These show up as small badges and filters, and they're often the exact thing someone is searching for.

6. The Q&A section

Anyone can post a question here, including you. Seed it yourself with the three questions you get asked most on the phone ("Do you take walk-ins?", "Do you have parking?", "Do I need a card to book?") and answer them as the business. It saves you the phone call and it's more content Google can match to a search.

7. Posts and review replies

A profile that posts now and then, and replies to every review, reads as an active business to both Google and a customer deciding between you and the shop next door. It doesn't need to be often — once a week is plenty — but it needs to not be empty.

What to do with this list

Go through it top to bottom once, properly, and it holds for months. After that it's five minutes a week: a new photo, a reply to a review, maybe a post. If you'd rather hand the whole thing off, that's the Google profile care we do for shops across New Port Richey and Pasco County.
