How to ask for Google reviews without being pushy

Most happy clients never leave a review. It’s not that they don’t care. They walk out, the day carries on, and it never crosses their mind. Asking isn’t begging. It’s a small favor, asked kindly, at the right moment. Here are the words and the setup to do it without feeling pushy.

Three ground rules

  1. Ask once, and ask everyone the same way. A short, friendly ask beats a speech.
  2. Make it a ten-second job. A direct link or a QR code, not “find us on Google.”
  3. Never pay for reviews or trade a discount for one. Google says offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited (Google’s tips to get more reviews). It can put your whole profile at risk.

First, get your link and your QR code

Google lets you create a link and a QR code that takes a client straight to your review box. Their page shows how to create a Google link or QR code to request reviews. Save the link in your phone’s notes so you can paste it into a text in seconds. Customers need to be signed in to a Google account to post, which is worth knowing if someone says it’s not working.

At the chair or the front desk

The best moment is right after the client sees the finished result and smiles. Wait until the work is done, not while they’re still deciding if they like it.

Salon: “I’m so glad you love it. If you have a minute, a Google review helps new clients find us. There’s a code right here, or I can text you the link so it’s there when you get to your car.”

Barbershop: “Good to see you, man. If the cut came out how you wanted, a quick Google review is the best thing you can do for the shop. Scan this, it takes a few seconds.”

If you’re busy: “Can I text you a link to leave us a review? No rush at all.”

Skip the ask if the client is rushed, upset, or unsure about the result. Fix that first. Don’t hold up a phone or stand over someone while they type.

A QR code at the counter

Print the QR code with one plain line next to it, like “Liked your visit? Scan to leave us a Google review. Thank you!” Put it at eye level where clients wait or pay: by the register, at the mirror, or on the card they take with them. Scan it yourself with your phone before you print a hundred copies, and check it from a few feet away.

The text after the visit

(The names below are made up. Use your own.) A text sent the same evening catches people when the visit is fresh. Keep it short, in your own voice, and only send it to clients who gave you their number and are happy to hear from you. Send it once. If they don’t answer, let it go.

“Hi Maria, it’s Dana from the shop. Thanks for coming in today! If you liked your visit, would you leave us a quick Google review? Here’s the link: [link]. No worries if not. See you next time!”

“Hey Jordan, thanks for stopping by. If you’ve got a minute, a Google review really helps a small shop: [link]. Appreciate you.”

Replying to reviews

Every reply is public, and the next client reads it. Google’s advice is to keep replies short, personal and conversational rather than promotional, and to avoid sending the same “thank you” to everyone. You can read and reply from your Business Profile (here’s how).

Again, made-up names, so you can see the tone.

A good review

“Thank you, Maria! It was a pleasure doing your color, and I’m glad you like the warmth in it. See you at your next appointment. – Dana”

A bad review

“I’m sorry your visit wasn’t what you expected, Jordan. That isn’t how we want anyone to leave. Please call me at the shop so I can hear more and make it right. – Dana”

With a bad review, stay calm and don’t argue. Don’t share anything private about the client. Offer to talk by phone and let others see you handled it well.

What to leave out

  • No discounts, freebies or gift cards in exchange for a review.
  • No asking staff, friends or family to write ones that aren’t a real visit.
  • No reminders every week. One ask is plenty.
