Field notes

Why isn’t my shop showing on Google Maps?

You search for your own service, standing in your own shop, and the map shows three other places. Here are the eight usual reasons, in the order we check them, and what to do about each one.

1. Your profile isn’t verified, or it got suspended

Open your Google Business Profile (opens in a new tab). If it says “Get verified” or shows a suspension notice, nothing else on this list matters yet. Finish verification first. Suspensions often come from keywords stuffed into the business name, or an address Google can’t confirm.

2. Your main category is wrong

The first category carries the most weight. A barbershop listed as “Hair salon”, or a lash studio listed as “Beauty salon”, can miss the exact search a customer types. Pick the most specific main category, then add a few more that truly fit.

3. Your hours are missing or wrong

Google is less likely to show a shop it thinks is closed. Check your regular hours, and set holiday hours before every holiday. Wrong hours also lead to angry reviews.

4. There’s a duplicate listing

An old listing from a past address, or one a former owner made, splits your reviews and confuses Google. Search your shop’s name and phone number on Google Maps. If you find a second listing, use “Suggest an edit”, then “Close or remove”, and choose “Duplicate”.

5. Your name, address and phone don’t match everywhere

Google compares your listing with your website, Facebook, Yelp, Apple Maps and directory sites. “Ste 4” in one place and “Unit 4” in another is fine. A different phone number or an old address is not. Our checklist for name, address and phone walks through it.

6. The customer is too far away

Distance is one of the three things Google weighs, and it’s the one you can’t change. A shop two miles away will usually beat you for someone standing next to it. What you can do is be the best match for people near you, and have a website that names the towns you serve.

7. Your profile looks thin

Few photos, no services listed, no description and no recent reviews all tell Google the profile isn’t looked after. Add real photos of your work and your storefront, list every service, and ask happy clients for reviews every week.

8. You have no website, or it doesn’t say what you do

Google reads your website to understand what you offer and where. A Facebook page helps, but a simple website that names your services and your town gives Google much more to go on. Read do you need a website if you have Instagram?

Quick test. Search for your service plus your town on your phone, for example “barbershop New Port Richey”. Then search your exact shop name. If your name search finds you but the service search doesn’t, the problem is usually 2, 5, 7 or 8.

Questions

Why doesn’t my shop show up on Google Maps?

The most common reasons are an unverified or suspended profile, the wrong main category, missing or wrong hours, a duplicate listing, or a name, address and phone that don’t match across the web. Distance matters too: Google favors shops closest to the person searching.

How long does it take to show up after I fix things?

Small changes like hours and photos usually show within a few days. Verification and bigger fixes can take a few weeks. Google doesn’t promise a timeline.

Can I pay Google to rank higher on the map?

No. Ads can appear above the map, but the regular map results can’t be bought. They depend on relevance, distance and how well known your shop is.

Sources: Google Business Profile Help, “Tips to improve your local ranking on Google” and “Verify your business on Google”.
